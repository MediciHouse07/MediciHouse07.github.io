Why the Hessian Matrix Was Invented

Calculus 2026-07-05 17:36 JST
Subject shelfBook StudyHome

Study Wrap-Up

We studied the Hessian matrix through Simmons's second derivative test, but focused on the deeper question: why did people need a matrix at all? The answer is that one-variable curvature has one direction, while two-variable curvature has infinitely many directions, including mixed diagonal behavior.

Visual Map: Hessian Determines Local Shape

1. The One-Variable Problem Was Too Simple

One variable

At a critical point, the first derivative vanishes. The second derivative gives the local shape.

f′(a)=0,f″(a)>0⇒minimum

Two variables

At a critical point, both first partial derivatives vanish, but the surface can bend differently in different directions.

fx=0,fy=0
Reason for invention: the Hessian matrix packages every second-order direction clue into one object, so we do not have to check directions one by one forever.

2. The Direction Quiz That Revealed the Need

We compared three functions at the origin. Looking only along the axes was not enough.

f1=x2+y2,f2=x2-y2,f3=2xy
FunctionAlong y=0Along x=0Along y=xAlong y=-xMeaning
f1x^2y^22x^22x^2bowl in every checked direction
f2x^2-y^200up in one direction, down in another
f3002x^2-2x^2mixed term hides from the axes
Aha: 2xy looks flat on the coordinate axes, but it is a bowl on one diagonal and upside-down on the other. That mixed behavior is exactly what fxy records.

3. What the Hessian Stores

The matrix

H=[fxxfxyfyxfyy]

Pure second partials tell the coordinate-axis bending. Mixed partials tell how the variables bend together.

Local shape formula

Near a critical point, the quadratic part is controlled by the Hessian.

f(a+h,b+k)≈f(a,b)+12[hk]H[hk]

4. The Three Hessians From the Quiz

Bowl

H1=[2002]

Positive curvature in both coordinate directions, no mixed twist.

Saddle

H2=[200-2]

Positive in x, negative in y. This cannot be a local minimum or maximum.

Hidden saddle

H3=[0220]

The axes look flat, but the mixed entries reveal diagonal curvature.

5. Simmons's Test in Modern Hessian Language

Simmons states the second derivative test using the discriminant D. In modern notation, this is the determinant of the Hessian.

D=fxxfyy-(fxy)2=det(H)
ConditionClassificationShape idea
D > 0 and fxx > 0local minimumcurves upward in all directions
D > 0 and fxx < 0local maximumcurves downward in all directions
D < 0saddle pointup in some directions, down in others
D = 0test inconclusivemore direct analysis is needed
Your observation: if D>0, then fxx and fyy have the same sign. So checking fyy>0 with D>0 also confirms a local minimum.

6. Application: Least-Squares Best-Fit Line

The Hessian becomes useful outside pure graph-sketching. For data fitting, it can certify that the error surface is a bowl, so the critical point is truly the best fit.

Problem setup

Fit a line y=mx+b to the points (0,1), (1,2), and (2,2).

S(m,b)=(b-1)2+(m+b-2)2+(2m+b-2)2

Gradient equations

Sm=10m+6b-12=0
Sb=6m+6b-10=0

Hessian of the error

H=[10666]
D=10⋅6-62=24>0

Since Sbb=6>0 and D>0, the error surface is bowl-shaped.

Best-fit line

Solving the two linear equations gives:

m=12,b=76
y=12x+76

The Hessian tells us this critical point is not a saddle or a maximum; it is the minimum-error line.

7. Mental Model

Without Hessian

Check the x-axis, y-axis, one diagonal, the other diagonal, and still worry that another direction is hiding something.

With Hessian

Collect the second-order information once, then use determinant and sign rules to classify the local shape.

Short version: the Hessian is a direction-organizer for curvature. It is the two-variable replacement for the single number f″(a).
