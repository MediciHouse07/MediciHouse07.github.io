1. Practice Question

∫xe2xdx

The product contains a polynomial factor and an exponential factor, which is a standard signal to try integration by parts.

2. Learner's Answer

14(2x−1)e2x
Assessment: correct antiderivative. Add +C because an indefinite integral represents a family of antiderivatives.

3. Integration by Parts Setup

Use the formula

∫udv=uv−∫vdu

Differentiate the polynomial

u=x,du=dx

Differentiating x simplifies it to one.

Integrate the exponential

dv=e2xdx,v=12e2x

The factor 12 compensates for the inner derivative of 2x.

4. Carry Out the Calculation

1

Substitute

Insert u,du,v into the integration-by-parts formula.

2

Integrate again

The remaining integral is only an exponential integral.

3

Factor

Factor out 14e2x to match the compact answer.

∫xe2xdx=x2e2x−12∫e2xdx
=x2e2x−14e2x+C
=14(2x−1)e2x+C

5. Verify by Differentiation

Differentiate the proposed antiderivative using the product rule:

ddx[14(2x−1)e2x]=14[2e2x+2(2x−1)e2x]
=xe2x

The derivative returns the original integrand, confirming the answer.

Final Takeaway

Two habits to keep: choose u so differentiation simplifies it, and always append +C to an indefinite integral.
