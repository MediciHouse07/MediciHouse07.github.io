Sample Mean: Expectation, Variance, and the n=1 Confusion

Socratic Practice Wrap-Up

The main confusion was not algebra. It was a clash between two different meanings of “sample variance”: the variance of the sample mean as a random variable versus the variance calculated from observed sample values. The n=1 case exposed the distinction.

1. The Object in the Formula Is a Random Variable

Define the sample mean

Yn=1n∑i=1nXi

Yn is not one already-observed number. It is a random variable: repeat the experiment with fresh samples and the resulting sample mean changes from run to run.

The key distinction: uppercase Yn means the random variable; lowercase yn can denote one realized value.

2. Why the Expectation Does Not Depend on n

For identically distributed samples with population mean μ:

E[Yn]=1n∑i=1nE[Xi]=1nnμ=μ

The factor 1n and the n equal expectations cancel. Therefore the center stays fixed for every sample size.

n = 1

Y1=X1, so E[Y1]=E[X].

Large n

The expectation is still the same population mean. Large n does not improve the center itself.

3. The n=1 Expectation Puzzle

The statement

E[Y1]=E[X]

does not mean one actual draw equals the population mean.

If a distribution takes 0 or 10 with equal probability, then

E[X]=5

but one realized draw is either 0 or 10. The expectation describes the center of the distribution across repeated draws; it is not a claim about one realized observation.

4. Why Variance Shrinks Differently

For independent samples with population variance σ2, variance scales with the square of a constant:

Var(aX)=a2Var(X)

Therefore:

Var[Yn]=1n2∑i=1nVar(Xi)=1n2nσ2=σ2n

Expectation gets one factor of 1n; variance gets 1n2. That is why one factor of n remains in the denominator.

5. The n=1 Variance Puzzle

At n=1:

Var[Y1]=Var(X)

This does not mean one observed number has the population variance. It means the random variable produced by repeatedly taking one draw has exactly the same distribution as X, so it has the same variance.

What caused the confusion: “variance of the sample mean” was being read as “variance inside one realized sample.” Those are different objects.

6. Sample Variance Is a Different Quantity

The usual sample variance computed from observed values is

s2=1n−1∑i=1n(xi−x¯)2

This is an estimator of the population variance. It is not the same thing as

Var(Yn)=σ2n

Sample variance s²

Spread among observed sample values; estimates the population variance.

Variance of sample mean

Spread of the sample mean across repeated samples of size n.

7. Why Large n Becomes Useful

The expectation remains fixed:

E[Yn]=μ

but the variance and standard deviation shrink:

Var[Yn]=σ2nSD[Yn]=σn

So larger n does not move the center. It makes the distribution of the sample mean tighter around that center. A realized sample mean is therefore increasingly likely to be close to E[X].

Final mental model: large n does not make the expectation “more equal” to the population mean; it makes a random realized sample mean more reliable because the spread around that mean shrinks.

8. LLN and CLT Placement

The expectation and variance identities above hold for every n under the stated i.i.d./independence assumptions; they are not “large-n-only” formulas. Their large-n consequence explains the intuition behind the Law of Large Numbers: the sample mean concentrates around the population mean. The Central Limit Theorem goes further by describing the approximate shape of the standardized sample-mean distribution for large n.
