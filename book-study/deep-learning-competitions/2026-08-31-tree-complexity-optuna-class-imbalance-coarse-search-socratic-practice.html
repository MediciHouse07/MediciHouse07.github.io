1. Tree Depth and Leaf Count Control Complexity

For a Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT), deeper trees or more leaves can represent more specific interactions. Too much complexity can overfit; too little can underfit.

Shallower / fewer leaves

Less capacity; may miss useful structure.

Deeper / more leaves

More capacity; may fit noise and hurt Cross-Validation (CV).

Practice result: if training performance stays excellent while CV becomes worse as max_depth rises, overfitting is the leading diagnosis.

2. Optuna After a Coarse Search

The workflow practiced here was: first identify a promising region with a broad/coarse search, then let Optuna spend the limited trial budget on a narrower fine search.

Coarse stage

Find a useful neighborhood, for example learning rate 0.03–0.08 and depth 5–8.

Fine stage

Use Optuna to explore the promising neighborhood more efficiently.

Avoid: throwing away strong coarse-search evidence and reopening an unnecessarily huge search space when the trial budget is small.

3. scale_pos_weight for Class Imbalance

scale_pos_weight increases the contribution of positive-class errors to the training objective. It does not create new positive samples.

A common count-based starting point is:

scale_pos_weight≈NnegativeNpositive

With 19,000 negative and 1,000 positive samples:

190001000=19
Tiny-test answer: 19.

4. English: “Coarse Search” vs “Roughly Search”

Coarse search is a natural technical noun phrase for an initial low-resolution or broad search. Rough search is understandable but less technical. Roughly search is better treated as a verb phrase, such as “we roughly searched the range.”

Preferred tuning language: coarse search → fine search, or broad search → narrow/fine search.
