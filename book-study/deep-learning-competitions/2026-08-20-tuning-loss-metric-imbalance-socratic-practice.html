1. Data Augmentation: Keep the Label True

The checklist’s rule is simple: augmentation is useful only while the transformed sample still supports the same label. Weak augmentation may not regularize enough; reasonable augmentation increases variety; excessively strong augmentation can remove the evidence that made the label valid.

Too weak

Little extra variety, so memorization remains easy.

Reasonable

More valid views of the same underlying example, improving generalization.

Too strong

The transformed sample may no longer justify the original label.

Socratic takeaway: augmentation is not “more transformation is better”; the label-validity constraint comes first.

2. Hyperparameter Tuning: Build the Measuring Instrument First

Before tuning, fix the random seed and build reliable cross-validation. Then find a reasonable learning-rate region, change one variable at a time, narrow the search, and finally test the chosen setting across multiple seeds.

Reliable CV + fixed seed
Find LR region
Change one variable
Fine search
Multi-seed check

Why fixed seed?

Reduce random variation so a comparison is more likely to reflect the hyperparameter change itself.

Why CV before tuning?

Without a trustworthy measuring instrument, an apparent improvement cannot be separated cleanly from noise.

Small gains need verification. A CV improvement around 0.001 on one seed may be noise; the checklist recommends multi-seed confirmation.

3. Loss and Metric Have Different Jobs

The training loss is the quantity used to generate gradients for parameter updates. The competition metric is the final judging rule. They can point in the same direction, but they do not have to be identical.

Loss

Used inside training and backpropagation. Standard neural-network losses such as MSE or cross-entropy are chosen so gradients can be computed.

Metric

Used to judge model quality. It can be a hard rule such as F1, Accuracy, AUC, or mAP and does not need to serve directly as the differentiable training objective.

For mean squared error:

L=1n∑i=1n(yi−y^i)2

Its differentiability is not the interesting competition problem by itself. The important question is whether lowering that loss also improves the metric the leaderboard cares about.

Key distinction: a valid differentiable loss can keep improving while the competition metric stays flat.

4. Why F1 Introduces a Threshold

F1 balances precision and recall, not accuracy and recall:

F1=2Precision·RecallPrecision+Recall

A classifier often outputs probabilities. F1 needs hard positive/negative decisions, so probabilities must first be converted with a threshold.

Choose threshold
Hard labels
Precision + Recall
F1

Lowering the threshold usually predicts more positives: recall tends to rise while precision may fall. Raising it usually does the opposite. This is why the checklist says threshold-based metrics should be tuned on OOF predictions instead of blindly fixing the threshold at 0.5.

Example: moving a positive prediction from 0.60 to 0.90 can improve cross-entropy loss while leaving the hard label unchanged at threshold 0.5; F1 may therefore remain unchanged.

5. Confusion Matrix: Sick = Positive, Healthy = Negative

6. Supplemental Statistics Connection: Type I and Type II Errors

This connection came from the Socratic discussion rather than from the checklist itself. Under the conventional hypothesis-testing setup where H0 means “healthy / no disease,” and a positive prediction corresponds to rejecting H0:

FP = Type I error

A healthy person is flagged as sick: reject the null when the null is true.

FN = Type II error

A sick person is predicted healthy: fail to reject the null when the null is false.

The mapping depends on how the null hypothesis and positive class are defined; with the medical convention above, the correspondence is exact.

7. What We Can Now Say Without Memorizing

Training

Use a differentiable loss to generate gradients; tune with reliable CV; verify small gains across seeds.

Evaluation

Inspect the metric the competition actually uses. For F1, the probability threshold is part of the evaluation pipeline.

Imbalance

Accuracy can be misleading when one class dominates. The minority-class errors must be inspected directly.

Confusion matrix

TP, TN, FP, and FN are not mere labels: they reveal what type of mistake the model is making.
