Cramer's Rule and Determinants

What This Note Captures

This note wraps the Socratic study thread on Cramer's rule: why the two-by-two determinant is ad-bc, why determinant zero means no unique solution, and why the higher-dimensional formula works by replacing one column.

1. Start from the System

For two variables, the system is:

ax+by=ecx+dy=f

In matrix form:

[abcd][xy]=[ef]
Key interpretation: the answer vector is made by mixing the columns of the matrix.

2. Where Does ad-bc Come From?

It comes from elimination. To solve for x, make the y parts equal, then subtract.

multiply first equation by d
multiply second equation by b
subtract to remove y
coefficient of x becomes determinant
adx+bdy=debcx+bdy=bf
(ad-bc)x=de-bf

So the denominator in Cramer's rule is not magic. It is the leftover coefficient after elimination.

det[abcd]=ad-bc

3. Cramer's Rule in Two Variables

The rule says: to solve for one variable, replace that variable's column by the right-hand side column.

Original determinant
det(A)=ad-bc
Replace first column for x
x=det[ebfd]det(A)
Replace second column for y
y=det[aecf]det(A)

4. What If the Determinant Is Zero?

Your intuition

You said: determinant zero means singular, and the system does not have enough formulas to solve the variables.

Refined version

For a square system, the number of written equations may equal the number of variables. But the equations are not independent, so there are fewer useful equations than variables.

det(A)=0⇒no unique solution

That can mean infinitely many solutions or no solution. It cannot mean exactly one solution.

5. More Variables: Same Pattern

For a three-variable system, write the matrix by columns:

A=[a1a2a3]

The equation Ax=b means:

b=xa1+ya2+za3

To get x, replace the first column by b:

det(b,a2,a3)=xdet(a1,a2,a3)

The other terms disappear because they create repeated columns, and a determinant with repeated columns is zero.

General rule: xk=det(Ak)det(A), where Ak replaces the k-th column of A with b.

6. Stats 110 Bridge: Independent Information

The same thinking appears in probability and statistics. A formula, statistic, or equation is only useful if it contributes independent information.

Bernoulli MGF memory

In the earlier Stat 110 thread, one parameter p determined the Bernoulli MGF, expectation, second moment, and variance.

Linear system memory

In Cramer's rule, independent equations determine the unknowns. Dependent equations collapse the determinant to zero.

Statistics translation

Singularity often means the data or model cannot identify a unique answer. More rows on paper do not help if they repeat the same information.

unique solution needs independent information

7. Socratic Checkpoints

Checkpoint 1

When deriving ad-bc, why do we multiply the first equation by d and the second by b?

Checkpoint 2

For x+y=2 and 2x+2y=5, are the equations dependent? Does the system have infinitely many solutions or no solution?
