Symmetric Matrices: Why Eigenvectors Can Be Chosen Orthonormal

1. Starting Point: Orthogonal

Two vectors are orthogonal when their inner product is zero:

qTv=0

Geometrically, this is the algebraic test for perpendicular directions.

2. The Symmetric-Matrix Step

Let S be symmetric, so S=ST. Suppose

Sq1=λ1q1,Sq2=λ2q2

Look at the same scalar in two ways:

Use the second eigenvector

q1TSq2=λ2q1Tq2

Use symmetry and the first eigenvector

q1TSq2=(Sq1)Tq2=λ1q1Tq2

Therefore

(λ1−λ2)q1Tq2=0

3. What If the Eigenvalues Are Different?

If λ1≠λ2, the first factor is nonzero. Therefore the inner product must be zero:

q1Tq2=0
Conclusion: for a real symmetric matrix, eigenvectors belonging to distinct eigenvalues are automatically orthogonal.

4. What If the Eigenvalues Are the Same?

If λ1=λ2, then the previous equation becomes

0×q1Tq2=0

That equation tells us nothing about the angle between the two vectors. So arbitrary eigenvectors with the same eigenvalue are not automatically orthogonal.

Important distinction: “can be chosen orthogonal” is not the same as “every eigenvector is born perpendicular to every other eigenvector.”

5. Why We Can Repair the Repeated-Eigenvalue Case

Suppose u and v have the same eigenvalue λ:

Su=λu,Sv=λv

For any scalars a and b,

S(au+bv)=λ(au+bv)

So linear combinations stay inside the same eigenspace and remain eigenvectors with eigenvalue λ.

1

Choose a basis

Take independent eigenvectors spanning the repeated-eigenvalue eigenspace.

2

Gram-Schmidt

Replace that basis by orthogonal linear combinations of the same vectors.

3

Stay in the eigenspace

Because the replacements are linear combinations, they are still eigenvectors for the same λ.

6. From Orthogonal to Orthonormal

Orthogonal only means perpendicular. To make the vectors orthonormal, divide each one by its length:

qi←qi‖qi‖

Now each vector has unit length and different chosen vectors are perpendicular:

qiTqj=δij

7. Why the Eigenvector Matrix Q Is Special

Put the carefully chosen orthonormal eigenvectors into the columns of

Q=[q1q2…qn]

Then diagonal entries of QTQ are qiTqi=1, while off-diagonal entries are zero. Therefore

QTQ=I,Q−1=QT

Final Takeaway

Distinct eigenvalues

The associated eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix are automatically orthogonal.

Repeated eigenvalue

Arbitrary eigenvectors need not be orthogonal, but an orthogonal basis can be constructed inside that eigenspace.

The sentence to remember: for a symmetric matrix, we carefully choose an orthonormal eigenbasis. Not every possible eigenvector is automatically perpendicular to every other possible eigenvector.
