Gram-Schmidt from Projection: Socratic Derivation

Linear Algebra 2026-08-13 07:55 UTC+8

Socratic Practice Wrap-Up

The goal was to derive Gram-Schmidt from a single idea: remove from each new vector the part already pointing in the directions we have kept. The leftover is perpendicular; normalization then turns that perpendicular vector into a unit vector.

1. The Goal

Start with two independent vectors a1 and a2. We want new vectors that span the same space but are orthogonal.

q1=a1‖a1‖

The first vector is simply normalized.

2. Derive the Second Direction

Instead of memorizing a projection formula, suppose the new vector has the form

q2*=a2−cq1

Choose c so that the leftover is perpendicular to q1:

q1T(a2−cq1)=0
q1Ta2−cq1Tq1=0

Because q1 has unit length, q1Tq1=1. Therefore

c=q1Ta2
The coefficient was not guessed. It was forced by the requirement that the leftover be orthogonal.

3. Projection and Perpendicular Error

The term

(q1Ta2)q1

is the projection of a2 onto the direction q1. Subtracting it leaves the perpendicular error:

u2=a2−(q1Ta2)q1

Projection

The amount of a2 already pointing along q1.

Error

The part left after subtraction, perpendicular to q1.

4. Recover the Familiar Formula

The remembered projection formula often starts from an unnormalized vector u and another vector v. Set

q1=u‖u‖

Substitute this into the normalized projection form:

v−(q1Tv)q1=v−uTvuTuuuTu

The two square-root denominators multiply:

uTuuTu=uTu

So the same orthogonal leftover becomes

u2=v−uTvuTuu
Same idea, different notation: if the direction vector is already normalized, the denominator disappears. If it is not normalized, the denominator uTu corrects for its length.

5. Normalize Only After Orthogonalizing

The leftover u2 is perpendicular, but it may not have unit length. So Gram-Schmidt finishes the second direction with

q2=u2‖u2‖

This separates the two operations cleanly:

1

Project

Find the part already lying in an old direction.

2

Subtract

Keep only the perpendicular error.

3

Normalize

Rescale the error to length one.

6. The Third Vector: Remove the Whole Plane

For a third vector a3, subtract the projections onto both existing orthonormal directions:

u3=a3−(q1Ta3)q1−(q2Ta3)q2

Geometrically, the two projection terms are the part of a3 lying in the plane spanned by q1 and q2. The leftover points perpendicular to that plane.

q3=u3‖u3‖

7. General Gram-Schmidt Pattern

Once q1,…,qk−1 are orthonormal, process the next vector by subtracting every old projection:

uk=ak−∑j=1k−1(qjTak)qj
qk=uk‖uk‖
If uk=0: the new vector was already completely inside the span of the earlier vectors, so it adds no new independent direction.

Final Takeaway

Gram-Schmidt is repeated projection subtraction. Each new vector is decomposed into a part already explained by the old directions plus a perpendicular error. Throw away the explained part, keep the error, and normalize it.
new orthogonal direction=original vector−all previous projections
